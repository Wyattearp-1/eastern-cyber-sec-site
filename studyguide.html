🛡️ What Happened Here?

You encountered a classic spear-phishing attack. Notice the subtle cues:

  • Urgency: "@school.edu account for AP Cybersecurity class" creates false specificity
  • Familiar branding: Mimics a trusted interface to lower guard
  • Context manipulation: Links the login to your current class to seem legitimate

🚩 Red Flags to Watch For:

  • Check the URL: Was this really accounts.google.com? Or something lookalike?
  • Unexpected urgency: "Verify now" or "Account suspended" messages often mean trouble
  • Unsolicited requests: Legitimate services rarely ask you to log in via email/link
  • Request for sensitive info: Real companies won't ask for passwords in forms they send you

In real-world scenarios, attackers could use this data to access your email, school accounts, or worse. This simulation helps build muscle memory for spotting these tricks.

✅ How to Protect Yourself:

  • 🔍 Always hover before clicking links to see the real destination
  • 🔐 Enable MFA everywhere — even if they get your password, they still can't log in
  • 🏢 When in doubt, navigate directly to the service instead of clicking links
  • 📧 Report suspicious emails to your IT/security team
Ethical Use Notice: This simulation is designed for educational purposes only. Never use phishing techniques without explicit authorization from the target organization. Unauthorized phishing is illegal and violates cybersecurity laws worldwide.
